# Brand Discovery Skill

The BELIEF methodology and the principles that make it work. This skill codifies the institutional knowledge of how Wasson Enterprise conducts brand discovery.

## Core Philosophy

**Brands are discovered, not invented.** The client already knows who they are. WE's job is to make the implicit explicit — to give structure, language, and visual form to something that already exists inside the business. The process draws out identity; it does not impose one from the outside.

Every step that doesn't serve that function is overhead. Cut it.

## The Three Constraints

Every step in BELIEF is governed by three hard constraints. If you violate any of them, the process breaks.

1. **You can't synthesize what you haven't gathered.** The live session is only as good as the inputs going in. Client homework, materials, and WE research must be substantially complete before the session.

2. **Strategy must precede expression.** You cannot design a logo, build a color palette, or write messaging until the brand foundation is locked. Visual identity is downstream of strategic decisions. Skipping ahead is the most tempting shortcut and the most expensive one.

3. **Synthesis requires decision-making authority in the room.** The live session produces real-time strategic decisions. If the person who can say "yes, that's us" isn't present, the session produces recommendations instead of decisions, and the timeline doubles.

## The Live Session: Four Stages

### Stage 1: WE DISCOVERY (60-75 minutes)

**Purpose:** Situation analysis and SWOT deep dive.

**What happens:**
- Open session: introductions, agenda walkthrough, ground rules
- Present pre-filled SWOT starter (built from homework and research) — client reacts, corrects, and deepens
- Facilitate differentiation mapping exercise
- Document current vs. desired brand state
- Confirm and lock objectives for the session

**What to surface:**
- Where WE's research was wrong — corrections from the leadership team are valuable signals
- Strengths the team takes for granted (they don't see them as differentiators)
- Weaknesses they're aware of but haven't addressed
- External opportunities they haven't considered
- Threats they're not tracking

**Known process hurdles:**
- SWOT discussions spiral into operations vs. brand — keep redirecting to brand implications
- Time lost re-explaining the process to attendees who didn't prep
- Not getting answers to key questions, causing follow-up meetings and longer delivery
- Running out of time in correlation to agreed scope of work

**Facilitation notes:**
- This stage is about validation and calibration. WE presents, the client reacts.
- The most valuable moments are when the client pushes back — it means you've touched something real.
- Watch for topics that generate energy (leaning in, animated discussion) vs. topics that fall flat.

### Stage 2: WE EXPERIENCE (60-75 minutes)

**Purpose:** Competitive positioning and market landscape.

**What happens:**
- Present competitive landscape analysis
- Walk through target audience profiles and signals
- Review market and industry trends
- Facilitate discussion: where does the brand fit?
- Identify white space opportunities collaboratively

**What to surface:**
- How the leadership team perceives competitors (often different from market reality)
- Which competitors they respect and why (reveals values)
- Which competitors they dismiss and why (reveals blind spots)
- Where they believe they're differentiated (test against research)
- Customer segments they prioritize and deprioritize (reveals strategic intent)

**Known process hurdles:**
- Clients get defensive about existing visual identity — approach with respect, not critique
- WE team may lack deep client-industry context in real time — thorough pre-session research mitigates this
- Getting too into the weeds or wordsmithing when the focus should be high-level brand foundation elements — save detailed language work for post-session

**Facilitation notes:**
- This stage is where the therapy session begins. Discussing competitors often surfaces emotional responses that reveal brand values.
- The white space conversation is critical — it's where positioning decisions start forming.
- Watch for disagreements within the leadership team — these are the tensions that need resolution.

### Stage 3: WE INTERPRET (45-60 minutes)

**Purpose:** Brand foundation development.

**What happens:**
- Workshop Vision statement (collaborative drafting)
- Workshop Mission statement
- Define Core Values through facilitated exercises
- Develop Brand Positioning statement
- Identify Brand Pillars and Reasons to Believe

**What to surface:**
- The words the leadership team naturally uses when they're passionate (their authentic language)
- Values they live vs. values they aspire to (honesty here is critical)
- The promise they're willing to make (and defend)
- What they want to be known for in 5-10 years

**Known process hurdles:**
- Conflicting opinions among client leadership on brand direction — this is valuable tension, not a problem
- Session fatigue by Stage 3 reduces quality of input — energy management is critical, consider a break before this stage

**Facilitation notes:**
- This is the hardest stage. The team must make decisions, not just discuss possibilities.
- Resist the urge to let them wordsmith — capture the intent, refine the language later.
- When they struggle to articulate something, ask "what are you NOT?" — defining the negative often reveals the positive.
- The positioning statement is the single most important output. Spend the time to get it right.
- Use "react mode" — present AI-generated draft options for the team to react to rather than starting from blank. People critique better than they create from scratch.

### Stage 4: WE EXPRESS (30-45 minutes)

**Purpose:** Path forward and alignment.

**What happens:**
- Present the path forward framework
- Review deliverable timeline (Phase 1, Phase 2, Phase 3)
- Align on immediate next steps and ownership
- Capture final questions and commitments
- Close session

**What to surface:**
- Energy level and commitment — are they excited about the direction?
- Any lingering doubts or unresolved tensions
- Practical constraints (timeline, budget, organizational politics) that affect execution
- Who the internal champion will be for brand adoption

**Known process hurdles:**
- Visual direction language (adjectives) can be too subjective — many back-and-forth discussions to get it right
- Disagreement on logo style slows progress — defer detailed creative decisions to Phase 2
- Messaging frameworks sometimes disconnect from visual identity — ensure strategic coherence
- Next steps and timelines not documented in real time — facilitator must capture commitments as they happen

**Facilitation notes:**
- End on energy. They should leave feeling heard and excited, not exhausted.
- Confirm the key decisions made during the session — no ambiguity.
- The handoff to Phase 1 should feel seamless, not like starting over.

## Engagement Mode Differences

### new-brand

**Session emphasis:**
- Stage 1: Focus on founder intent and aspirational identity rather than existing state
- Stage 2: Extra time on competitive landscape — they need to find their space
- Stage 3: More creative latitude — no existing brand to anchor against
- Stage 4: Excitement should be high — this is a beginning, not a correction

**Common dynamics:**
- Founders often have strong but unarticulated visions — draw it out
- Without existing brand constraints, sessions can drift — keep focus on decisions
- The team may not have thought about competitors systematically — the research presentation is often eye-opening

### rebrand-tweak

**Session emphasis:**
- Stage 1: Focus on what's changed — market, customers, capabilities — since the brand was last defined
- Stage 2: Focus on perception gaps — how the market sees them vs. how they see themselves
- Stage 3: Identify what to keep, what to evolve, what to discard
- Stage 4: Change management — how to evolve the brand without confusing existing customers

**Common dynamics:**
- Emotional attachment to the existing brand — respect it while guiding evolution
- "We just need to update the logo" syndrome — the team may underestimate what needs to change
- Internal disagreement about what's working and what isn't — surface these tensions early

### rebrand-pivot

**Session emphasis:**
- Stage 1: Confront why the current brand no longer fits — this requires honesty
- Stage 2: The competitive landscape has likely shifted — the pivot is often a response to this
- Stage 3: Build the new identity with intention — what do they want to be known for now?
- Stage 4: The pivot plan needs to address existing stakeholders — customers, employees, partners

**Common dynamics:**
- Grief for the old brand — even if the pivot is strategic, people are attached
- Fear of alienating existing customers — validate this concern, then work through it
- The pivot often reveals deeper strategic changes — be prepared for scope expansion
- Leadership alignment is especially critical — disagreement about the pivot direction is common

## Brand Foundation Elements — Definitions

These are the precise definitions used in the WE Brand Overview deliverable. Each element has a shorthand that helps clients internalize it.

| Element | The "..." | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Our "Why" | Fundamental reason for being beyond profit. The positive impact we are committed to making. |
| **Vision** | Our "Where" | The future we are working to create. What the industry looks like when we succeed. Our ultimate destination. |
| **Mission** | Our "What & How" | What we do every single day to achieve our vision. Our core function and commitment. |
| **Core Values** | Our "Guiding Principles" | Principles that guide internal culture and behaviors. The rules we live by and the bedrock of who we are. |
| **Audience** | Our "Who" | The specific people and organizations we serve, speak to, and build relationships with. |
| **Positioning** | How We're Seen | Strategic statement defining our unique place in the market relative to competitors and in audience minds. |
| **Pillars** | Our "Promises" | Foundational themes and core promises we make to our audience that bring positioning to life. |
| **Reasons to Believe** | Our "Proof" | Tangible, evidence-based claims (hard and soft) that support brand pillars and prove we deliver. |

These definitions should inform how `/belief:5-distill` frames its output — use the "Our Why/Where/What" language to make elements intuitive for client teams.

## The Art of Brand Distillation

The ultimate output of BELIEF is distillation: taking a half-day of rich, complex conversation and reducing it to a few statements that everyone believes in.

**What makes great brand distillation:**
- **Authenticity** — the statements feel like the company, not like marketing
- **Specificity** — they couldn't belong to anyone else
- **Memorability** — people can recall and repeat them
- **Conviction** — the leadership team reads them and says "yes, that's us"
- **Actionability** — they provide clear guidance for decisions

**The distillation process:**
1. Capture the raw material from the session (notes, recordings, whiteboards)
2. Identify the through-lines — themes that appeared in multiple stages
3. Find the emotional core — what did the team feel most strongly about?
4. Draft candidate statements — multiple options, each capturing a different angle
5. Test against the Three Questions: Is it true? Is it unique to them? Would they fight for it?
6. Refine until the statement passes all three tests

**Common pitfalls:**
- Generic statements that could apply to any company ("We're passionate about innovation")
- Aspirational statements disconnected from reality (the team wouldn't believe them)
- Committee-written statements that try to include everything and say nothing
- Clever wordplay that sounds good but means nothing
